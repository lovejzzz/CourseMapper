**Thesis:** V0.13.5 gave CurriculumOS _knowledge_ — 86 quote-verified concepts
across 10 disciplines, real readings, cited pedagogy. V0.14 gives it
_judgment_: the genome stops merely _storing_ cited facts and starts
_reasoning about teaching_. A textbook lists what's true. A chatbot generates
something plausible. Only CurriculumOS can look at a whole course against a
whole verified knowledge graph and say, with receipts: "your Week 6 builds on
something you never taught — here's the missing piece, cited," and "every
lesson in this course maps to this competency at this Bloom level."

The moat is unchanged and load-bearing: judgment built on quote-verified
anchors is _defensible_ where the same judgment from a language model is just
a confident guess. We already detect prerequisite gaps deterministically
(`prerequisiteAudit.js`); what we lack is the layer that **acts** on them and
makes them **visible**, plus the competency crosswalk institutions actually
pay for.

## P1 — Prerequisite-gap diagnosis & cited bridge injection

Today `auditPrerequisites` walks the genome's `requires` edges against lesson
order and flags two defects — `missing-prerequisite` (a required concept no
lesson teaches) and `out-of-order` (taught after it's needed). The findings
surface only as a generation log line. P1 turns detection into judgment:

1. **Classify every gap by what the genome can do about it:**
   - **bridgeable** — the missing prerequisite _exists as a kernel in the
     genome_. The genome can fill the hole.
   - **assumed-background** — the prerequisite is named by a `requires` edge
     but no kernel exists (a foundational concept outside the current
     genome). The genome can only flag it, honestly.

2. **Cited bridge injection.** For every bridgeable gap, build a
   **prerequisite primer** from the missing kernel: its quote-anchored
   definition, one anchored fact, the citation, and a one-line "you need this
   for _{the lesson concept}_." Primers land as `Resource` entities
   (`origin: 'genome-prerequisite'`) so they render in supportingResources,
   the syllabus, and the Sources & Licenses appendix — and as a
   **Prerequisite check** note on the affected lesson plan. The hole isn't
   just flagged; it's filled, with receipts, at zero AI cost.

3. **Make it visible everywhere judgment belongs:** a `judgment` line in the
   run digest and `PACKAGE_MANIFEST.json`, a "Prerequisite Check" note on the
   affected lesson plan, primer resources in the syllabus appendix, and the
   generation log.

**Acceptance:** a course that teaches a concept whose genome prerequisite it
omits gets (a) a flagged finding, (b) a cited primer resource when the
prerequisite is in the genome, (c) a lesson-plan prerequisite note; a course
whose sequence is sound produces no findings; zero network, fully
deterministic.

## P2 — Competency & standards crosswalk

The crosswalk institutions pay consultants to build, generated from data we
own plus a clearly-sourced curated layer:

1. **`kernel.standards`** — a new normalized, optional field: an array of
   `{ framework, code, label, url }`. Authored like attribution (curated,
   never model-asserted), seeded small and honestly — we do not fabricate
   authoritative ABET/AACN mappings we cannot defend. The _mechanism_ is the
   deliverable; the seed proves it.
2. **`competencyMap.js`** — maps each course concept to its **Bloom level**
   (owned: every kernel carries `bloomCeiling`) plus any tagged framework
   codes. Produces a per-outcome competency table.
3. **Render a "Course Competency Map"** in the syllabus: each learning
   outcome / key concept → Bloom level → framework codes, with a short note
   that the mapping is generated from the course's verified concepts.
4. **`knowledge:audit`** verifies every standards-framework URL resolves
   (link health, same as shard books and pedagogy DOIs).

**Acceptance:** the crosswalk renders in the syllabus for any genome-linked
course; Bloom coverage is computed from kernel data; seeded standards tags
render with working links; the audit covers them.

## P3 — The judgment surface

Tie P1 and P2 into one visible "this course was reasoned about" surface:

1. A **Course Judgment** block in the run digest and `PACKAGE_MANIFEST.json`:
   "N prerequisites assumed · M bridged with cited primers · Bloom span
   X–Y · K concepts mapped to standards."
2. Cited prerequisite primers flow into the existing workspace trust-strip
   "cited sources" count (they are `genome-prerequisite` resources), so a
   bridged gap shows up where reviewers already look.
3. **`tests/judgment-proof.test.js`** — the before/after harness: fixture
   courses with deliberate prerequisite holes, proving detection, bridge
   injection (cited), no-false-positive on sound courses, and competency
   coverage, all offline.

**Acceptance:** the judgment numbers render in the digest and manifest; the
proof harness is green; existing golden-equivalence and output-artifact gates
stay green.

## Cost & constraints

- Generation cost unchanged: P1/P2/P3 are deterministic graph reasoning over
  data we already ship. No new model or network calls on the compile path.
- The trust gate stays sovereign: primers are built only from quote-anchored
  kernels; standards tags are curated and link-checked, never model-asserted.

## Explicitly deferred

- **Embedding-based semantic linking.** A true embedding linker needs to
  embed the instructor's query _at runtime_, which means either a runtime
  model (violates "the compile never blocks on the network") or a heavy WASM
  model in the bundle. The architecturally honest path is **build-time
  surface expansion** — a key-gated foundry pass that proposes additional
  aliases/related terms per kernel, baked into the shard, so the runtime
  linker stays purely lexical with a richer surface set (the same
  model-proposes/gate-disposes pattern as `proposeKernels.mjs`). Deferred to
  a later release; the current lexical+alias linker already resolves 100% of
  the four flagship courses.
- **Cross-discipline prerequisite gaps at depth.** Detecting that a nutrition
  course silently assumes thermodynamics requires foundational math/physics
  shards the genome does not yet have. P1 handles every gap the _current_
  genome's `requires` edges express; richer cross-discipline assumptions
  arrive with those shards.
- **Adaptive/learner-context compilation** (remedial vs. honors vs. ESL) and
  the **misconception-causation graph** remain on the intelligence roadmap.

## Why this is the release that earns "most intelligent"

Coverage is table stakes — more textbooks link marginally more courses.
Judgment is the differentiator: knowing what a course forgot to teach,
filling it with a cited primer, and mapping every lesson to a competency is
something no textbook does and no chatbot does _credibly_. Built on the trust
gate, CurriculumOS doesn't just know the material — it knows what your
students will be missing, and it can prove its reasoning.
