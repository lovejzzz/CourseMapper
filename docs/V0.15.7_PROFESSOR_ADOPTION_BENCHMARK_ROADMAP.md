# V0.15.7 - Professor Adoption Benchmark: real courses become the judge

_Written June 15, 2026. This roadmap extends v0.15.6's self-improving ruler
with real university course artifacts. The goal is not to claim that professors
approved CourseMapper. The goal is to make public professor-taught courses act
as a standing benchmark board so the autonomous quality loop can discover gaps
that synthetic fixtures and generic standards can miss._

## Recommendation

Build a 12-case professor-adoption benchmark now.

The current self-improvement loop is valuable, but its latest 500-round report
still says `repair-required`. It passed package integrity and standards-backed
checks, yet it continues to find compiler-level repair candidates:

1. replace cross-domain boilerplate with discipline-appropriate examples and
   student work products,
2. replace generic "course evidence" phrasing with actual source artifacts,
3. reduce repetitive templated phrasing and add course-specific language where
   the source supports it.

That is exactly the gap a real professor benchmark should pressure. A package
can satisfy a rubric and still feel wrong to a professor because the deliverable
genre, classroom workflow, source evidence, grading texture, or course
operations do not match how the field is actually taught.

## How many professors or courses do we need?

Use three tiers.

| Tier                |                             Size | Purpose                                                                   | Gate                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Starter benchmark   | 12 public professor/course cases | Serious enough to expose cross-domain failures across the product         | Required before claiming "professor-adoption benchmarked"                                                                 |
| Release benchmark   |                         24 cases | Enough breadth to guard v0.16 course quality across major course families | Required before a major compiler or deliverable-quality release                                                           |
| Ambitious benchmark |                      40-50 cases | Enough diversity for a credible university-usability claim                | Required before any public claim that CourseMapper can produce university-ready packages without heavy instructor rewrite |

The 12-case starter is the right next goal. It is large enough to cover six
course families with two examples each, while still small enough for repeated
autonomous loops.

## Research thesis

The real adoption question is not:

> Did CourseMapper generate a complete package?

The adoption question is:

> Would an instructor looking at this package recognize their course, trust the
> disciplinary artifacts, and know exactly what they would teach, assign, grade,
> and hand to students next week?

To answer that without a human reviewer, the benchmark must compare
CourseMapper output against public source courses with real syllabi,
assignments, exams, labs, readings, lecture sequences, staff/support models, and
field-specific student work products.

## What the benchmark should extract

For each source course, create a source manifest with these fields:

1. `courseId`
2. `sourceUrl`
3. `publicInstructorNames`
4. `artifactTypesAvailable`
5. `disciplineFamily`
6. `courseModality`
7. `primaryStudentWorkProducts`
8. `assessmentArchitecture`
9. `supportAndOperationsModel`
10. `domainVocabularyMustAppear`
11. `domainVocabularyMustNotBeGeneric`
12. `policyBoundaries`
13. `sourceLimitations`
14. `benchmarkQuestions`
15. `hardBlockers`

The source manifest is not a scrape dump. It is a compact contract that says
what a professor-realistic package must preserve.

## Adoption Benchmark Score

Use a 100-point score with hard blockers.

| Dimension                           | Weight | What it asks                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------- | -----: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Source fidelity                     |     20 | Does the output preserve the public course's actual artifacts, sequence, assessment structure, and evidence?                            |
| Discipline fit                      |     20 | Does the output sound and behave like the field: proof, code, lab, close reading, language practice, biomedical case, or large lecture? |
| Deliverable authenticity            |     15 | Are assignments, rubrics, quizzes, lesson plans, slides, and study guides real versions of the expected professor/student work product? |
| Instructor workload and editability |     15 | Could an instructor adapt this quickly, with clear assumptions and no hidden cleanup traps?                                             |
| Student learning clarity            |     10 | Would students know what to do, why it matters, and how they will be graded?                                                            |
| Course operations                   |     10 | Are support channels, deadlines, office hours, grading cadence, technology, submission, and staff workflows represented where relevant? |
| Accessibility and learner agency    |      5 | Does the package support multiple ways to engage, perceive, practice, and express learning?                                             |
| Package craft                       |      5 | Does it avoid repetition, template leakage, awkward generated phrasing, and export defects?                                             |

Pass thresholds:

1. `>= 85`: professor-adoption benchmark pass for the case.
2. `75-84`: usable with instructor revision; must become a repair candidate.
3. `< 75`: blocked.
4. Any hard blocker forces blocked regardless of score.

Hard blockers:

1. fabricated source, professor, reading, dataset, policy, or assignment,
2. wrong deliverable genre for the discipline,
3. generic template language where source-specific evidence exists,
4. invented institutional policy or safety rule,
5. inaccessible or broken exported artifact,
6. untraceable assessment/objective chain,
7. missing primary student work product for the course family.

## Judge rules by course family

### Math and quantitative courses

Required evidence:

1. named mathematical objects or methods from the source,
2. problem sets, exams, solutions, or proof tasks,
3. notation discipline,
4. worked examples or proof skeletons where appropriate,
5. grading criteria tied to correctness, reasoning, and communication.

Reject:

1. generic "analyze course evidence" language,
2. soft professional reflection replacing calculation/proof,
3. objectives that say "understand" without measurable work.

### CS and data courses

Required evidence:

1. code, notebook, dataset, or toolchain expectations,
2. edge cases, tests, debugging, or submission workflow,
3. lab/homework/project cadence,
4. support systems such as Ed, office hours, FAQs, or autograder notes when present in source.

Reject:

1. wet-lab language in data science,
2. programming assignments without input/output behavior,
3. project prompts without deliverables or grading criteria.

### Humanities and writing courses

Required evidence:

1. named primary or theoretical texts from the source,
2. interpretive questions,
3. textual evidence expectations,
4. paper prompts, revision, discussion, or section participation,
5. grading criteria for claim, evidence, analysis, organization, and style.

Reject:

1. generic discussion prompts with no text,
2. comprehension quizzes replacing interpretive writing where writing is central,
3. source-free claims about authors or theory.

### Language courses

Required evidence:

1. speaking/listening practice,
2. vocabulary and grammar progression,
3. script or pronunciation work where relevant,
4. cultural context,
5. oral interviews, dialogues, lab practice, quizzes, or in-class activities.

Reject:

1. language learning reduced to reading summaries,
2. missing oral practice,
3. unsupported cultural generalizations.

### Biomedical, health-science, and professional courses

Required evidence:

1. professional or clinical translation context,
2. case studies or applied problems,
3. domain vocabulary and methods,
4. ethics, safety, regulation, patent/FDA, patient perspective, or policy boundaries where source supports them,
5. research paper, homework, or exam architecture.

Reject:

1. invented clinical authority,
2. generic role-play replacing science/engineering evidence,
3. unsupported medical advice.

### Large lecture and online/hybrid operations

Required evidence:

1. onboarding flow,
2. schedule, deadlines, and weekly cadence,
3. staff/support model,
4. academic honesty or AI/tool policy where present,
5. submission, gradebook, office hours, community, or feedback workflow.

Reject:

1. polished content with no support operations,
2. missing tool setup for courses built around a toolchain,
3. teacher-adoption guidance that does not distinguish public OCW from local institutional delivery.

## Autonomous loop design

Add a `professor-adoption` lane to the self-improvement system.

Recommended commands:

1. `npm run audit:professor-adoption:research`
   - refresh public source manifests for the benchmark cases.
2. `npm run audit:professor-adoption:fixtures`
   - convert manifests into deterministic CourseMapper input fixtures.
3. `npm run audit:professor-adoption:judge`
   - compile packages and score each case against the Adoption Benchmark Score.
4. `npm run audit:professor-adoption:ledger`
   - write ranked JSON and Markdown findings.
5. `npm run audit:professor-adoption:repair-plan`
   - map the top findings to source modules, tests, and proof commands.
6. `npm run audit:professor-adoption:proof`
   - run before/after proof for implemented compiler repairs.

Recommended artifacts:

1. `verification-output/professor-adoption/latest.md`
2. `verification-output/professor-adoption/latest.json`
3. `verification-output/professor-adoption/source-manifests/*.json`
4. `verification-output/professor-adoption/packages/<case-id>/`
5. `verification-output/professor-adoption/findings/*.json`
6. `verification-output/professor-adoption/repair-candidates/*.md`

## Ledger schema

Every finding should be machine-readable. No human interpretation should be
needed to decide the next action.

```json
{
  "caseId": "mit-1806-linear-algebra",
  "sourceUrl": "https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/",
  "dimension": "discipline-fit",
  "severity": "P1",
  "scoreImpact": -12,
  "artifact": "lesson-plan",
  "lessonOrWeek": "week-03",
  "sourceExpectation": "problem sets, matrix methods, worked solutions",
  "observedOutput": "generic evidence-analysis activity",
  "failureClass": "generic-template-leak",
  "suspectedOwner": "src/lib/courseBlueprintCompiler.js",
  "requiredRepairAction": "use discipline-specific work-product vocabulary when source course family is math",
  "acceptanceCriteria": [
    "generated assignment names a mathematical object or method",
    "rubric criteria judge correctness and reasoning",
    "no generic course-evidence phrase appears in math artifacts"
  ],
  "proofCommands": [
    "npm run audit:professor-adoption:judge -- --case mit-1806-linear-algebra",
    "npm run test -- tests/v0157-professor-adoption.test.js"
  ]
}
```

## First implementation scope

Do not try to implement all 12 as full production fixtures on the first pass.
Build the loop in this order:

1. Add source manifests for all 12 cases.
2. Implement the scorer and ledger using three smoke cases:
   - `mit-1806-linear-algebra`,
   - `berkeley-data8-fa25`,
   - `yale-engl310-poetry`.
3. Fix the current known compiler issues if the benchmark reproduces them:
   - discipline-specific examples,
   - real source artifact names,
   - repetitive generated phrasing.
4. Expand to the remaining nine cases.
5. Make the 12-case run the required local proof for any future claim about
   professor-adoption quality.

## How this should change CourseMapper

The benchmark should push the project toward five product capabilities.

### 1. Discipline packs

CourseMapper should have source-grounded discipline packs for math, CS/data,
humanities, languages, biomedical/professional, and large-course operations.
Each pack defines expected work products, vocabulary, artifact genres, and
review rules.

### 2. Source-truth compiler

The compiler should extract and preserve actual source artifacts: problem sets,
proof tasks, notebooks, datasets, close-reading essays, oral interviews,
research papers, exams, support channels, and tool workflows.

### 3. Adoption simulator

The internal judge should ask "what would block an instructor from adopting
this next week?" instead of only asking "does this look complete?"

### 4. Course operations layer

For large or tool-heavy courses, the output must include operational details:
office hours, staff model, submissions, deadlines, grade release, academic
honesty, technology setup, and student support.

### 5. Honest external-review bridge

The public benchmark is not real instructor approval. It prepares the package
for real review by making the instructor-review packet more targeted. When real
instructors become available, they should review packages already filtered by
this benchmark.

```text

Run one professor-adoption benchmark cycle for the 12 public cases. Refresh the

readable findings ledger, and select the highest-leverage repair that does not
require human interpretation.

If a repair is needed, implement the smallest code change, add focused tests,
rerun the affected professor-adoption cases, and report before/after evidence.
Do not claim professor approval. Do not commit or push unless I explicitly ask
after the local proof is complete.
```

## Best prompt for a 100-round version

Use this when the infrastructure exists:

```text
Run a 100-round autonomous professor-adoption quality loop.

Each round must select one public benchmark case or mutation from
docs/V0.15.7_PROFESSOR_ADOPTION_BENCHMARK_ROADMAP.md, compile the CourseMapper
package, score it with the Adoption Benchmark Score, write JSON/Markdown ledger
entries, select a repair only when the ledger identifies a reproducible P0/P1
or high-leverage P2, implement that repair with tests, and rerun the affected
case plus regression cases.

Stop early only for a real blocker: missing source access, failing core gates,
or a repair that cannot be made without human policy judgment. At the end,
report trend movement, fixed findings, remaining findings, new regressions,
and whether the loop is still discovering useful defects.
```

## Release claims allowed after v0.15.7

Allowed only if implemented and proved:

1. CourseMapper now benchmarks generated packages against 12 public
   professor-taught university courses.
2. The benchmark scores source fidelity, discipline fit, deliverable
   authenticity, instructor workload, student clarity, operations,
   accessibility, and craft.
3. The benchmark writes machine-readable findings and repair candidates that do
   not require human interpretation.
4. The benchmark distinguishes public-source adoption pressure from real
   professor approval.

Forbidden:

1. "Professors approve this."
2. "University-ready without instructor review."
3. "External validation complete."
4. "The public course authors endorsed CourseMapper."

## The big vision

CourseMapper should become a course engineering system, not a course-content
generator.

The next leap is a closed quality loop:

1. public professor courses define benchmark reality,
2. standards define the quality floor,
3. the autonomous judge finds adoption blockers,
4. the compiler repairs the blocker,
5. before/after proof shows whether quality moved,
6. the release contract says exactly what was proved and what was not.

If we do this, CourseMapper can improve without waiting for humans while still
being honest that final classroom trust belongs to real instructors.
