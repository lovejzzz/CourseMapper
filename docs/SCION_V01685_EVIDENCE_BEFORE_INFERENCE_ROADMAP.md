# Scion V0.16.85 — Evidence Before Inference

## Goal

Ship one understandable public Scion product that uses the strongest source-grounding work from the Algi research prototype internally, prepares trustworthy lesson evidence before browser-local inference, preserves an explicit privacy boundary, avoids duplicate work, and completes a professor-facing browser and export audit before the release is called finished.

Success means:

- the landing page offers Scion as the sole free product model;
- legacy experimental model settings migrate to Scion;
- unresolved lessons can receive admitted source evidence before local authoring;
- private mode sends no course-topic research request;
- opt-in research states what may leave the device and where it may be sent;
- one verified evidence pass does not trigger a second discovery pass;
- the compiler, Agent, workspace, and export surfaces retain one Scion identity;
- automated gates pass without upgrading their result into an instructor or classroom claim; and
- the deployed site completes a real course, Agent interaction, clean-console check, and physical ZIP audit at desktop and phone-size viewports.

## Lane 1 — One public Scion

Remove the experimental Algi route from the landing-page model selector while preserving old project compatibility. The provider keeps one no-key model option, `Scion V0.16.85`. A saved `algi-v0` model id resolves to the current Scion option instead of reviving a retired choice.

The internal evidence modules may retain historical implementation names until a later low-risk refactor, but active user-facing copy, status events, settings, workspace identity, Agent identity, and export handoff must say Scion.

## Lane 2 — Evidence before inference

Run a source-consolidation prepass only for lessons the shipped Curriculum Genome did not fully resolve. The prepass may use uploaded or local knowledge in private mode and consented current sources in research mode. It must not schedule a second foundation model or introduce another public model download.

Evidence admission is fail-closed:

- at least three complete lesson facts;
- at least three named concepts;
- at least one trusted citation;
- fully anchored concept provenance;
- HTTPS for remote source URLs; and
- lesson-owned evidence only.

Instructor-provided facts remain first priority. Specialized source ledgers for supported language and literature cases remain second. The Scion evidence overlay fills only the unresolved boundary. Accepted evidence becomes the immutable numbered source ledger used by Scion's existing local-provider contract.

## Lane 3 — Explicit privacy

Current-source research is off by default. Private mode makes no course-topic request. The opt-in control explains that only the course title and uncovered lesson topics may be sent to DOAJ, Europe PMC when relevant, and Wikipedia.

The evidence engine can be internal; network behavior cannot be secret. A denied storage context remains private. Turning the setting off removes the stored consent flag.

## Lane 4 — One bounded research transaction

If Scion has already researched or reused cached evidence for the current lessons, skip the older open-reading and source-finder discovery transaction. Preserve verified receipts and reading resources already attached by the evidence path. This prevents redundant provider traffic, avoids a misleading second progress sequence, and shortens the optional research path without lowering admission standards.

## Lane 5 — Honest evaluation

Required local evidence:

- focused evidence/configuration/forecast/binding tests;
- the complete unit suite;
- the complete Chromium browser suite;
- formatting and lint;
- production build and bundle ceilings;
- the 38-case main evaluation profile;
- the 14-course PR compiler contract; and
- the release-history truth audit.

These gates prove encoded behavior and regressions. They do not prove universal factual correctness, instructional effectiveness, accessibility in every context, instructor approval, classroom outcomes, paid-model superiority, or a trained-adapter win.

A measured content-quality lift requires matched same-course packages with and without the new evidence path. Until that evidence exists, V0.16.85 may claim the architecture, strict admission, reduced duplication, and passing contracts—not a new benchmark victory.

## Lane 6 — Professor-facing product proof

After merge and deployment, use the production origin so the existing public-model cache can be reused. Inspect:

1. desktop landing and expanded configuration;
2. phone-size light and dark landing states;
3. private and opt-in evidence forecasts;
4. course-map generation and each Living Course Compiler stage;
5. final Course Map and all nine compiled material families;
6. visible wording, status color, overflow, progress continuity, and action ownership;
7. one grounded Agent question;
8. the single ZIP download path;
9. browser warnings and errors; and
10. the physical ZIP, manifest, quality report, source receipts, archive integrity, and internal-name leakage.

Any release-blocking defect found in production gets a code fix and a new patch deployment before the goal is closed.

## Release Boundary

V0.16.85 includes:

- one public Scion model identity;
- migration of the retired experimental model id;
- the private Scion evidence prepass;
- strict evidence admission and provenance preservation;
- the explicit Scion current-source research control;
- evidence binding across native and prose authoring prompts;
- duplicate research suppression;
- visible acronym normalization in coverage forecasts;
- regression tests, release metadata, and complete documentation; and
- post-deploy browser, Agent, console, and physical-export verification.

V0.16.85 does not include:

- changed Gemma weights;
- activation or promotion of the optional trained adapter;
- a second downloadable local model;
- silent course-topic network requests;
- a claim that the former zero-weight route should remain a standalone product;
- a funded GPT-5.4 mini rerun;
- independent instructor or classroom validation; or
- a claim that automated readiness has crossed its evidence ceiling.
