# V0.17.12 Learner Checkpoint

Date: August 3, 2026

## Goal

Make the generated work teach and assess the capability named by the lesson. A learner assigned automated testing must receive executable testing work; a learner assigned a policy memo must receive a real policy decision architecture. Preserve honest scoring and inspect the exported files, not only internal objects.

## Lane

Course-neutral artifact-genre inference, assignment and rubric alignment, executable learner assets, evidence and anchor projection, statistical-claim repair, Office rendering, wide-map print behavior, deterministic replay, and bounded release evidence.

## Why V0.17.11 was not enough

The V0.17.11 package was a strong evidence-system checkpoint, but independent content review scored its instructor content at 55 and its formatting at 74. The corrected 2:1 content/format composite was 61. Its most important defects were semantic rather than cosmetic:

- the lesson titled “Functions and automated tests” accepted a generic PDF, slide deck, or recording and did not require executable tests;
- the “Integrative policy memo capstone” behaved like a generic analysis project and omitted the decision maker, options, stakeholder and equity effects, tradeoffs, implementation, and monitoring;
- assignment evidence packets and anchor samples existed in structured data but did not reliably reach the learner-facing DOCX;
- confidence-interval copy used an overexact “90 out of 100 samples” shortcut;
- one pitfalls-slide title rendered above the slide canvas, rubric and milestone blocks could begin a page without their labels, and a ten-column Course Map was forced onto one page.

The architectural mistake was allowing broad course mode and generic artifact defaults to outrank the capability explicitly named by a lesson. V0.17.12 reverses that order: the local lesson signal owns the assignment genre, and the selected genre owns its non-negotiable evidence.

## Implemented learner contract

### Automated testing

An automated-testing lesson now compiles as a code lab. The learner must submit executable source or a notebook, record how to run it from a clean start, preserve an initially failing test and the passing result after repair, exercise a typical case plus a boundary or error case, and explain the defect, code change, and verification. The package bundles `starter_policy_analysis.py`, `test_starter_policy_analysis.py`, a synthetic data file, and an automated-testing lab guide. The bundled suite executes three checks and fails closed on a missing required column.

### Policy memo

An explicit policy-memo lesson now compiles as a policy brief. Its brief requires the public problem, affected population, decision maker or authority, at least two feasible options, named evidence, stakeholder and equity effects, costs or constraints, tradeoffs, a recommendation and evidence limit, implementation ownership and timing, and a material risk with mitigation or monitoring.

### Evidence visible to learners

Assignment DOCX files now render their assigned evidence packet and anchor-sample revision contrast. Rubric DOCX files render both array and object forms of anchor examples. Exact lesson identity stays in headings and reverse stamps while repetitive body references compact to local, grammatical aliases.

### Honest concepts and readable files

The content boundary detects and repairs both “At 90% confidence…” and abbreviated “at CL = 90%…” overclaims. Pitfalls titles are shortened and capped for LibreOffice’s Georgia metrics. Wide Course Maps use landscape scaling and repeat the header plus identity columns instead of forcing ten columns onto one page. Anchor examples and milestone checklists remain grouped with their labels across page breaks.

## Release Boundary

V0.17.12 is a learner-readiness engineering checkpoint once the exact deployed commit and a fresh production ZIP reproduce these gates. It demonstrates course-neutral assignment alignment, executable starter work, deterministic package conformance, and inspected Office rendering for the retained six-lesson course. It does not establish universal factual correctness, independent instructor approval, accessibility certification, classroom outcomes, learned model-weight improvement, or parity with a paid reference model.
