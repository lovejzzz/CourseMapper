## Goal

Treat Scion as a learner: improve how it finds, retains, applies, checks, and revises evidence without embedding the facts or vocabulary of one course. The acceptance floor is a fresh-package score of at least 60/100, grounding of at least 15/25, assessment coherence of at least 10/15, and zero P0/P1 findings.

## Lane

Course-neutral source research, provenance-preserving claim memory, lesson-evidence revision, rendered-artifact grounding, assessment coherence, deterministic scoring, physical export verification, and honest release evidence.

## Architectural finding

The 34-point ceiling was not principally a formatting problem and was not proof that Gemma alone was incapable. Scion discarded useful source identity between stages, restarted research instead of learning from its portfolio, and graded package wiring rather than the claims and assessment evidence visible in exported files. This made stronger model prose easy to lose and weak output difficult to diagnose.

V0.17.11 changes the learning loop:

1. **Observe:** build a claim portfolio from retrieved source bytes, preserving canonical source ID, title, URL, locator, exact supported claim, and admission result.
2. **Plan:** compare that portfolio with the course and lesson evidence needs. Research is selected by domain and unresolved concept family, not by a hard-coded course corpus.
3. **Compose:** use admitted claims in lesson kernels while preserving citation identity. Integrative work may reuse already admitted evidence, but cannot manufacture a new support relationship.
4. **Check:** extract visible text from the generated DOCX, PPTX, and text artifacts. A claim earns grounding credit only when the exported wording can be bound back to the exact source receipt.
5. **Revise:** preserve successful lesson evidence, target unresolved lessons, and accept a revision only when the support ledger strictly improves.
6. **Assess:** reconstruct objective → task → student evidence → rubric criterion relationships from the final package. Alignment credit depends on the evidence students are actually asked to produce.

## Course-neutral implementation

- `algiKernelComposer.js` composes exact source-supported statements through general grammatical and claim-boundary rules rather than course-specific facts.
- `algiResearchPlan.js` and `algiResearch.js` select provider order and source-title families from course and lesson context.
- `sourceLedger.js` binds claims to the complete retrieved-snapshot digest, source identity, locator, quote byte offsets, quote hash, and rendered artifact hash, and rejects any mismatched chain.
- `blueprintEnrichmentPass.js`, `scionEvidenceLayer.js`, and `nativeGraphAuthoring.js` preserve canonical citations through enrichment and material assembly.
- `exportRenderedTextAudit.js` reads visible Office text; `packageZipExporter.js` reconstructs support and assessment receipts from the assembled archive.
- `assessmentCoherence.js` evaluates whether objectives, assigned tasks, expected student evidence, and rubric criteria agree.
- `automatedReadinessSignal.js` awards bounded positive evidence points and retains separate curriculum, texture, and integrity dimensions.

The initial exact-commit audit found five audited-course retrieval branches. They were removed before release. Generic noun-phrase canonicalization and coarse domain qualifiers now operate uniformly across unseen programming, linear-algebra, and environmental-policy topics; no course title, lesson sequence, fact, or source quotation from the test course remains embedded as a product rule. The optional adapter remains inactive and the public Gemma weights are unchanged.

## Roundtable disposition

Session `681bd065-f174-4971-80f1-1fecb81ed21c` ran six design rounds plus two completion audits against repository code. The room converged on provenance-preserving claim memory, portfolio-aware retrieval, exported-byte verification, assessment evidence chains, a preserve/revise scheduler, and a scorer separated from generation. Both completion audits reported zero material concerns for the implementation plan.

That design session was followed by exact-code review. The first pass found three P1 blockers; a later pass correctly rejected digest-and-offset metadata that omitted the source bytes and found one stale proof note that still treated 95 as accepted. The final code includes replayable snapshot bytes and a standalone ZIP verifier, and the proof note now treats 95 only as rejected history. V51 and V52 remain immutable rejected rulers. V53 binds grader 1.15.9 and the independently replayable implementation without inheriting an earlier score.

Session `d1a89853-bf12-4373-b88b-c05f94426df6` then spent six more rounds trying to falsify commit `50db5720`. It succeeded: a self-consistently re-signed manifest could separate a genuine quote from a fabricated claim, assert five assessment checks without replaying them, relabel an inadmissible source, or omit evidence rows while retaining a pass. The room correctly returned **CHECKPOINT NO**. The post-room implementation now extracts visible Office text independently, requires exact claim/quote identity, replays source admission and all five assessment predicates, pins objectives plus assessment identity in the external contract, and binds the physical ZIP, evidence bundle, score ledger, counts, score, grader version, and contract into one release attestation. Eight adversarial verifier tests cover byte tampering, semantic re-signing, source-policy forgery, assessment forgery, wrong contract roots, and evidence omission. A post-remediation exact-commit audit remains required before checkpoint certification.

Roundtable also recommended decomposing the large exporter into immutable artifact-bundle phases and moving longstanding domain relevance policies into declared adapters. Those are sound P2 architecture follow-ups; they do not replace the fresh production and external-attestation gates for this release.

The work also exposed one Roundtable defect: it sent Claude an extra live prompt as an authentication probe before the real turn. Roundtable v0.0.0.43 removes that duplicate provider request, trusts the bounded native account-status check for readiness, degrades once on a real turn failure, and rechecks automatically before the next discussion.

## Release Boundary

The local result clears the requested quantitative floor, but it is not yet the checkpoint. The checkpoint additionally requires:

- full unit, lint, format, build, bundle, release-history, and evaluation gates;
- a pushed and deployed exact release commit;
- a fresh production generation whose physical ZIP independently clears ≥60 total, ≥15 grounding, ≥10 assessment coherence, and zero P0/P1;
- representative production DOCX/PPTX rendering;
- a final Roundtable audit of the exact commit and exact production ZIP hash.

Even after those gates, the claim remains bounded. This release does not establish universal factual correctness, independent instructor approval, accessibility certification, classroom outcomes, learned-weight improvement, or parity with a paid reference model.
