**Source analysis:** the v0.13.1 live-run educator audit of the first enriched
production package (Introduction to Astronomy, 12 lessons, $0.11). The run
proved the pipeline; the audit graded the _education_ and found six ranked
gaps. This release closes all six. Verification:
`tests/astronomy-shard-proof.test.js` re-runs the audited course as a fixture
and asserts every gap, end-to-end through the real linker → blueprint →
compiler path.

## G1 — Sources and citations: the astronomy shard

`scripts/foundry/sources/astronomy-openstax.json`: **12 source-anchored
concepts** from OpenStax Astronomy 2e (CC BY 4.0) — diurnal motion, celestial
sphere, seasons/axial tilt, moon phases, Kepler's third law, the
electromagnetic spectrum, spectral lines, telescope light-gathering power,
stellar parallax, apparent magnitude, the solar nebula, and Hubble's law.
Every definition and fact anchors to a **verbatim quote fetched from the live
book** (June 10, 2026) and passes the foundry's mechanical quote-verification
gate. Two genuine archetype instantiations join Layer 2: apparent magnitude
as `epistemic/operationalization` and the solar nebula's angular-momentum
story as `method/conservation-balance`. `inferCourseDisciplines` routes
astronomy vocabulary to the shard. Genome: **7 disciplines, 49 concepts.**
Proof: the audited course resolves ≥7 of 9 fixture lessons from the genome
with "OpenStax Astronomy 2e" source labels on key terms — at zero AI cost.

## G2 — Worked examples: math bought once

- Genome: `workedExamples: [{problem, steps[], result, anchor?}]` on concept
  kernels (kernelSchema normalization + foundry admission). Shipped on
  Kepler's third law (Mars: P=1.88 yr → a=1.52 AU), parallax (p=0.1″ → 10
  pc), apparent magnitude (Δ2 mag → 6.25×), telescope aperture (4 m vs 1 m →
  16×), and Hubble's law (100 Mly → 2200 km/s).
- Model kernels: the contract gains an optional `workedExample` (wp/ws/wr
  compact keys) for quantitative lessons, parsed with completeness lint.
- Projection: `composeLessonFromConcepts` and `projectKernelToSurfaces`
  carry it into the lesson plan (rendered: problem → numbered steps → Result
  callout) and the study guide.

## G3 — Teaching scripts teach the content

`buildLessonPlanOutline` is kernel-aware: the warm-up becomes a
**misconception poll** (display the misconception, vote true/false, defend
with an observation — reveal the correction only after the vote); the
mini-lesson **works the worked example on the board** or builds from the
kernel's anchor fact; guided analysis opens with the kernel scenario. The
study guide's "what would strong work need to show" meta-question becomes
"explain why this is true and what evidence supports it: <kernel fact>".

## G4 — Misconception corrections corrected

Root cause: `composeLessonFromConcepts` dropped the genome's `corrective`
field, and the study-guide compiler paired every misconception with
`term: definition`. Now the corrective travels end-to-end (genome
`corrective` → keyTerm `correction` → study guide), the model-kernel
contract asks for `cx` (an explicit corrective statement, "NOT a restated
definition"), and the deterministic fallback phrases the definition as a
counter ("In fact: …"). Proof: the seasons misconception pairs with the
January-perihelion corrective.

## G5 — Concept maps render

The compiler attaches renderable `hub` + `spokes` (short kernel/concept
terms) to the keyTerm slide's visual descriptor — `slideVisual` now passes
them through — and the PPTX exporter prefers descriptor data over
bullet-derived phrases, whose full sentences almost never fit the spoke
guard (zero concept maps rendered in the v0.13.1 audit).

## G6 — Signature pedagogy: the observing protocol

`src/lib/observationProtocols.js`: when a course's own language promises sky
observation (night-sky, observing log, planetarium, telescope…), every
lesson plan carries a concrete protocol — six observing-log fields
(conditions, limiting magnitude, altitude in fists, sketch), a **weekly
naked-eye focus keyed to the lesson's concepts** (track a star's diurnal
motion; sketch moon phases over four nights; find a planet; compare star
colors), a Stellarium **cloudy-night alternative**, and observing basics
(dark adaptation, red light). Authored once as data, like throughline
profiles — the template for future field-observation families.
