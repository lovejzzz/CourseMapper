# V0.17.09 Roundtable Scoring Audit

## Scope

Six rounds and eighteen code-based turns reviewed `automatedReadinessSignal`, `deepQualityGrader`, package trust and export handoff, adoption verdicts, renderers, and their tests. The complete exported transcript is retained locally as `/Users/tianxing/Downloads/roundtable-2026-08-01 (1).md`.

## Release-blocking truth

The former score was not merely hard to explain; it was non-monotonic. With identical high-quality output, deleting an explicit ordered lesson sequence could change the curriculum branch, raise the numerator from roughly 35 to 45, shrink the denominator from 52 to 48, and promote the displayed band. Missing evidence improved the result.

The same architecture produced three other forms of false precision:

- correlated title and conformance observations earned credit in more than one construct;
- most deep-grader dimensions began at 100, so “no encoded defect fired” looked like positive validation;
- P0/P1 caps changed the overall score after aggregation without a serialized row that could reproduce the change.

The handoff path added two trust failures. Adoption logic scraped a report phrase that current report writers no longer emitted, and package trust used an unexplained `score < 85` threshold rather than named policy gates. The logical grading-scope digest also did not bind the rendered artifact bytes that the grader read.

## Decision

V0.17.09 ships two explicitly different constructs inside one canonical `SCORE_LEDGER.json`:

1. `deterministicPackageEvidence` has a fixed 100-point potential partitioned into earned, lost, and unobserved points. Unobserved is neither a pass nor a deduction.
2. `encodedDefectConformance` reports deterministic defect detection. Non-texture dimensions explicitly say that 100 means no encoded defect fired, not expert validation. Texture remains a direct content metric.

Every score-bearing rule has a stable ID and version, observed evidence references and fingerprints, an exact predicate, point buckets, recoverability, evidence tier, confidence basis, causal improvement action, dependencies, and named anti-gaming controls. One evaluated evidence ID may earn points in only one construct.

Conformance deductions preserve their nominal and effective point effects. Severity caps are replayable aggregate gates with predicate, transform, before, after, delta, reason, action, and triggering finding IDs.

The manifest binds the full ledger by SHA-256 and records a sorted path/byte/hash inventory of the exact grading input. Verification distinguishes verified, stale logical scope, invalid bytes or arithmetic, and unverifiable legacy data. Verified projections are immutable.

## What changed because of the audit

- Removed the `.69` transform and the moving 48/52 denominator.
- Removed title genericity and broad conformance double counting.
- Marked semantic grounding and assessment coherence unobserved until their actual constructs can be evaluated.
- Added specific “why” and “how to improve” text to rule rows, findings, the UI, and `QUALITY_REPORT.md`.
- Added stable finding/rule identities, exact deductions, replayable severity caps, and grader version 1.15.0.
- Added `SCORE_LEDGER.json`, manifest hash and artifact bindings, and structural verification.
- Removed report-prose scoring and the aggregate score trust threshold.
- Added monotonicity, recomputation, unique evidence ownership, tamper, stale, legacy, round-trip, and renderer regressions.

## Deliberately not claimed

The ledger is internally auditable, not externally authenticated. A malicious party able to rewrite every package object can also rewrite internal hashes; signatures or transparency receipts remain future work. Deterministic evidence is not a probability of classroom readiness, and empirical weights or bands still require blinded instructor judgments and classroom outcomes. Semantic grounding and human validation remain visible unobserved tiers until genuine evidence exists.
